[Fictional sample] Night-time Pedestrian Detection Pilot: Interim Summary

A six-month pilot in a mid-sized city tested whether roadside thermal cameras could warn drivers about pedestrians at unlit crossings. Twenty crossings were equipped with cameras linked to flashing LED signs placed 80 meters ahead.

During the pilot, the share of drivers who slowed below 30 km/h before the crossing rose from 34% to 61%. Near-miss events recorded by the cameras fell by about half. However, false alarms increased in heavy rain, when the cameras sometimes detected umbrellas or reflections as pedestrians.

The project team recommends three next steps: (1) combining thermal and radar sensors to reduce false alarms, (2) testing the system at school-zone crossings, and (3) developing a common performance test procedure so that products from different vendors can be compared fairly.
